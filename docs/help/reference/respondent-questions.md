# Questions respondents ask

The messages people send you while they are part-way through one of your forms, each one answered in
their language rather than yours.

Everything below the line is addressed to the respondent. Forward one section of it, or the whole
page, as it stands.

---

## "It says my progress was saved. Where has it gone?"

The form saves what you have typed as you go. A line at the foot of the page reads **Saving…** and
then **Progress saved**, and that is the whole of it — there is no account to make, no password, and
no email confirming anything.

What is saved belongs to this browser on this device. Open the same link again here and your answers
come back, under a line reading **We've brought back the answers you saved earlier.** Open it on
your phone instead, in a different browser, or after clearing your browsing data, and you get an
empty form. Nobody can send you a link back to your own half-finished answers, so if you might be
interrupted, finish where you started.

## "It says the form has changed since I saved. What do I do?"

Scroll through the form and fill in the blanks.

The message reads **This form has changed since you saved.** followed by a count of your answers that
need answering again. It means whoever sent you the link edited the form and published that edit
while your answers were sitting here. Everything that could still be put back has been put back. The
ones that could not are the ones whose question is no longer the same question, so your old answer
would no longer have meant what you meant by it.

Nothing has gone wrong, and nothing else you typed is affected. The form in front of you now is the
one that will be sent.

## "What does Not you? Start over do — does it delete what is there?"

It gives you an empty form, and it deletes nothing.

**Not you? Start over** sits beside **We've brought back the answers you saved earlier.** It is there
for a computer more than one person uses: a family tablet, a desk somebody else was at, a machine in
a library. If the answers that came back are not yours, press it and the page reloads with nothing
filled in.

What was saved stays saved. What pressing it does is stop this device offering those answers back to
you — there is no confirmation to click through, and no way to ask for them again from this page. So
press it when the answers are somebody else's, and not when you want to change one of your own. For
that, type over the answer.

## "The link will not open. What is it telling me?"

You get a plain page with one sentence on it, and the browser tab usually reads **Form unavailable**.
That sentence is the whole answer, and which one you got says whose problem it is and whether waiting
will help.

| The page says | What it means |
|---|---|
| **This form link was not found. Please check the link and try again.** | There is no form at that address. The link was deleted, or it lost characters on its way through an email or a chat message. Ask the sender for it again. |
| **This form hasn't been published yet. If you were sent this link, its author still needs to publish the form.** | The tab reads **Form not published yet**. The form exists and is not finished being made public. Waiting will not fix it; tell the sender. |
| **This form isn't open yet.** followed by a date, or **This form isn't open yet. Please check back later.** | The tab reads **Form opens later**. The form opens on a date the sender chose. Come back then. |
| **This form is no longer accepting responses.** | It has closed, either because its deadline passed or because the sender closed it. Only the sender can reopen it. |
| **This form has reached its response limit and is no longer accepting responses.** | The form took the number of answers it was set to take, and you were late rather than unlucky. Tell the sender if you still need to answer. |
| **Too many attempts from this network.** followed by how long to wait | Nothing to do with you personally. See the next section. |
| **This form link is not ready yet. Please try again later.** | The link has not been finished. Tell the sender. |
| **This form is receiving a lot of traffic right now. Please try again in a moment.** | Too many people at once. Wait a minute and open the link again. |
| **We could not open this form right now. Please try again later.** | Something at the far end failed. Try again in a few minutes; if it keeps happening, tell the sender. |
| **This form link is missing its form reference. Please check the link and try again.** | The address is incomplete. Ask for the whole link again rather than retyping it. |
| **Could not load the form. Please refresh the page or try again later.** | The page opened but the form itself did not arrive. Refresh once. |
| **Your browser does not support this form. Please use a modern browser.** | The browser is too old. Open the same link in an up-to-date one. |
| **We couldn't reopen your saved answers on this device. Start fresh, or request a link by email.** | Your earlier answers could not be brought back. You can still answer the form; you are starting from empty. |
| **This form is already open in another tab. Continue there, or start fresh here.** | You have the same form open twice. Go back to the other tab, or carry on here from empty. |

## "It turned several of us away at once, and we all work in the same building"

That is **Too many attempts from this network.** and a wait, and it is a cap on how often one
internet connection may open forms. An office, a school, a hotel or a conference is a single
connection as far as that cap is concerned, so a group of people opening the same link within a
minute of each other can hit it between them.

The page names how long to wait, and the wait clears on its own. Wait it out. Refreshing repeatedly
spends the budget again and makes the wait longer.

## "It will not let me submit. It says there have been too many submissions"

That is a different cap from the one above, and it is much friendlier than it looks.

You get a message at the top of the form, starting **Too many submissions. Please wait** and naming
a number of seconds. Everything you typed is still on the screen — nothing was lost and nothing was
sent. Wait the seconds out and press **Submit** again.

## "A dialog says my session has timed out"

It reads **Your session has timed out**, and under it:

> **For security, a form can only stay open for a limited time. Nothing you entered has been submitted, and you will need to enter your answers again.**

It means the form sat open too long — over lunch, or overnight in a tab you forgot about. It is not
a fault, and it is not about you. Press **Start again**, which reloads the form.

Read that middle sentence literally: your answers were not sent. If the form is a long one, the way
to avoid this is to fill it in and submit it in one sitting rather than leaving it open.

## "It will not take my file"

The form tells you why, in one line under the question, and only one of the reasons is worth trying
the same file again for.

- *The file is too big.* The message names the largest size the form will take. Nothing you do to the
  file in the form will shrink it — send a smaller one, or a version saved at a lower quality.
- *The file is the wrong kind.* The message names the type it refused and lists the kinds it does
  take, usually images, PDFs and text files. Save the file as one of those, or send it another way.
- **That file was not accepted. Try a different file.** A refusal for some other reason. The wording
  is the honest advice: the same file will fail the same way.
- **The upload did not go through. Please try again.** or **Upload failed. Check your connection and
  try again.** These two are the ones worth retrying. Press **Retry upload**.
- *Too many uploads in a short time.* The message names a number of seconds. Wait, then attach the
  file again.

None of this costs you the rest of your answers. The form is still there, still filled in, and you
can pick another file and carry on.

## "What is the security challenge?"

A box headed **Security challenge** appears under the last question on some forms. It is a check that
an actual person is filling the form in rather than a program, and for almost everybody it passes on
its own or after ticking one box. Then submit as normal.

Two things it can say:

- **Please complete the security challenge before submitting.** You pressed **Submit** before the
  check had finished. Wait for it, then press again.
- **The security challenge could not be loaded. Please refresh and try again.** The check could not
  reach your browser, usually a network or ad-blocker problem at your end. Refresh the page; if it
  keeps happening, try a different network or browser.

There is a third, and it is not something you can get past: **This form requires a security
challenge, but it has not been configured yet. It can't be submitted until the site owner adds a
Turnstile site key.** The form cannot be submitted by anybody in that state. Tell whoever sent you
the link — the *site owner* in that sentence is them, not you.
