# Preview on phone and tablet

The form exactly as a respondent gets it, at three widths, without publishing anything or sending
anyone a link.

![The preview taking over the screen, with the three widths to choose from along the top and the
form drawn at the narrowest of them, the size a phone would give it.](../images/preview-dialog.png)

## Before you start

- A form open in the builder. **Preview** is live from the moment the form loads, with no questions
  in it or with fifty, so you can open it at any point while you are building:
  [Add and arrange questions](../build/add-and-arrange-questions.md).

## Open the preview

1. Press **Preview** in the bar above the tabs, between **Save as template** and the publish
   control. It works from any tab.
2. A window opens, headed **Preview** and the form's name, with the form inside it. Answer it, move
   through it, press the buttons. Nothing you do in here is recorded as a response and nothing
   reaches your **Responses** tab.
3. In the middle of the bar are three width buttons — a screen, a tablet and a phone. The line
   beside them names the one you are on and gives its size: **Desktop** is whatever room the window
   has, **Tablet** is 820 pixels wide and **Mobile** is 393. Those are the widths a real iPad and a
   real iPhone report.
4. Press the phone. The line reads **Mobile**, and the form is not only narrowed but shortened to
   the height of a phone, so the fold falls where a respondent's fold falls — anything below it is
   something they have to scroll for.
5. If the browser window is too small to give the width you chose, the line adds **shrunk to fit**.
   You are then looking at something narrower than the device, so widen the window before you judge
   the layout.
6. Along the bottom, a form with more than one screen gets a row of buttons — **Welcome**,
   **Questions**, and one for each ending, named by its own title or simply **Ending** if you have
   not given it one. Press one to jump straight to that screen instead of answering your way there.
7. Close the window with the cross at the right of its bar, or press Escape.

## What to look for at phone width

A form that is comfortable on a laptop can be unusable on a phone — and a link sent in a message,
printed on a poster or scanned from a QR code is opened on one. The four things that go wrong:

- A long question that wraps to four lines and pushes the answer off the screen.
- A large title size, chosen because it looked confident on a desktop, that leaves no room for the
  first question.
- A background image that is handsome across a wide screen and illegible behind a narrow column.
- A page with so many questions on it that the **Next** button is a long scroll away. Splitting it
  is [Add sections and screens](../build/sections-and-screens.md).

The **Design** tab carries the same preview on its right-hand side, at whatever width that panel
has. It is the same machinery, so the two can never show you different things — but only this window
gives you the phone and tablet widths.

## What happens next

Nothing. A preview changes no data, records no response and publishes nothing.

Make it a habit before every publish: open the preview, switch to **Mobile**, and answer your own
form to the end. It costs a minute and it is the only check that catches a form which is correct and
unusable.
