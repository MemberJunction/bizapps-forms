# Reuse a form as a template

A form you have already got right, saved so that the next one starts where this one finished.

## Before you start

- A form worth reusing. A template is a whole form, so the time to save one is when the wording,
  the pages, the rules and the look are all settled — not on the first draft.
- Nothing else. You do not need to publish a form before you can save it as a template.

## Save a form as a template

1. Open the form and find **Save as template** in the bar above the tabs, between the render-mode
   buttons and **Preview**.
2. Press it. A small dialog opens, headed **Save as template**.
3. Check **Template name**. It arrives filled in with the form's own name, which is usually right.
   Change it if the template should be called something more general than this particular form —
   *Standard client intake* rather than *Acme intake, March*.
4. Fill in **What is it for?** if it needs one. It is optional, and it is the line under the name in
   the gallery, so it is worth the sentence: the placeholder shows the register — *e.g. Our standard
   client intake, with the consent block legal signed off*.
5. Read the note above the buttons, because it answers the question everybody has and nobody asks:
   **The whole form is copied — questions, pages, branching logic, validation, welcome and ending
   screens, theme, and any automations. Responses and share links are not.**
6. Press **Save template**, on the left. **Cancel**, on the right, closes without saving.

## Starting a new form from one

Templates are used from the **Forms** page, not from inside a form. Press **Templates**, at the top
beside **Author with AI** and **New form**, and a panel headed **Start from a template** opens under
the buttons. **Your templates** is the first group, newest first; **Starter templates** is below it.
Click a card and MJ Forms makes a new draft from it and opens it in the builder, ready to rename.

## The five starter templates

They are built in, so they cannot be edited or deleted, and every one of them makes a full form you
can change freely.

| Template | What it gives you |
|---|---|
| **Contact form** | **Name, email and a message — the general-enquiry default.** |
| **Event RSVP** | **Attendance, a guest count and dietary restrictions.** |
| **NPS / feedback** | **A Net Promoter Score question plus an open-ended follow-up.** |
| **Lead capture** | **A prospect's contact details and what they are interested in.** |
| **Application** | **A multi-section application with a resume upload.** |

## What happens next

The form you saved from carries a **Saved as template** chip in the bar above the tabs, so you can
tell at a glance that it is in the gallery. Keep editing it and that chip turns back into
**Save as template**, because the form and the template have diverged — saving again puts the newer
version in the gallery as its own template rather than replacing the old one. Two templates cannot
share a name, and MJ Forms says so rather than overwriting one.

A template is a copy taken at the moment you saved it, not a link back to the form. Editing the form
afterwards changes nothing in the gallery, and deleting a template changes nothing about the forms
already made from it — which is what MJ Forms tells you before it deletes one:
**This removes the template permanently. Forms already created from it are not affected — each one
was a full copy, not a link back to this template.**
