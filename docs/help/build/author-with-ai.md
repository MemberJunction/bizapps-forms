# Let AI draft a form

A form full of questions, written from a sentence describing what you need, and open in the builder
for you to fix.

## Before you start

- Nothing in MJ Forms. This is one of the three ways to start a form, alongside **New form** and
  **Templates**.
- A clear idea of what you are asking and of whom. The draft is only as good as the sentence you
  give it, and a vague sentence produces a vague form faster than you could have written a vague
  form yourself.

## Describe the form and generate it

1. Go to the **Forms** page and press **Author with AI**, at the top between **Templates** and
   **New form**. A panel opens under the buttons.
2. Write your brief into **Describe the form you want**. The placeholder is a good model of the
   length and the level of detail to aim for: *e.g. A 5-question event RSVP with dietary preferences
   and a guest count*.
3. Say three things in it if you can — roughly how many questions, what the form is *for*, and any
   specific answers you know you need. A count keeps it from writing twenty questions; the purpose
   decides the wording; the specifics are the things it cannot guess.
4. Read the line under the box, because it sets the expectation correctly:
   **AI drafts the questions; you can edit everything before publishing.**
5. Press **Generate**. It takes a few seconds. **Cancel** closes the panel without generating.
6. The new form opens in the builder with its questions already on the canvas. Nothing has been
   published and nobody can see it.

If you press **Generate** with the box empty, MJ Forms asks you to fill it in —
**Enter a brief describing the form you want.** — rather than guessing.

## What to expect from the draft

*It is a first draft, and you are the editor.* That is not a disclaimer; it is how to use the
feature well. What it saves you is the blank page and the typing, which is most of the work in a
form of fifteen questions. What it does not save you is the judgement.

Go through it once, in order, checking four things:

- *The wording.* The questions will be sensible and slightly generic. Yours are better, and
  rewriting one is a few seconds in **Question**.
- *The types.* A question that should be **Multiple choice** may have arrived as **Short text**,
  which costs you the ability to count the answers later. See
  [The 25 question types](question-types.md).
- *The options.* Where it wrote a list of answers, check the list is the one your organisation
  actually uses.
- *What it did not ask.* The thing you most need is the thing you did not think to put in the
  brief. It is easier to spot in a draft than in an empty form, which is part of why this is worth
  doing.

Then treat it as an ordinary form: make the questions you need compulsory, add the rules, and
publish when you are ready.

## What happens next

The draft is a form like any other, with nothing special about its origin. Every tab works on it,
every article in this section applies to it, and you can save it as a template once it is right.

If the draft is not close enough to be worth editing, the fastest move is not to argue with it.
Delete the form, write a longer brief naming the questions you know you want, and generate again.
