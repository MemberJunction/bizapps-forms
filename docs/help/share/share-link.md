# Create a share link

A web address you can send to anyone, which opens your form without a sign-in, an account or a
password.

![The Distribute tab showing a link: its web address ready to copy, and beneath it the settings that
govern the link — whether it is open, whether it asks for a captcha, how many responses it takes and
when it stops.](../images/distribute-link.png)

## Before you start

- A published form. A link to a form that has never been published opens to a message telling the
  respondent to wait for its author: [Publish a form](publish.md).

## Create the link

1. Open the **Distribute** tab. The first time, it holds a heading — **Share this form** — one line
   of explanation, and one button. The line reads: **Create a link and anyone can open this form —
   no sign-in needed. You also get a QR code and a snippet to embed the form in your own site.**
2. Press **Create a share link**. Nothing is asked of you: no name, no settings, no choices to make
   before you have seen anything. The link exists immediately and its badge already reads **Live**.
   There is no second step that turns it on.
3. Find it in the list down the left, under **Share links**. It is called **Share link** until you
   rename it, with its state and its running count underneath — `Live · 0 responses`.
4. The address itself sits in the box in the middle of the panel, with **Copy link** beside it. Press
   that and it changes to **Copied**. Under the box: **Send this to anyone. They open the form
   without signing in.**
5. Press **Open it yourself** before you send it anywhere. Your form opens in a new tab exactly as a
   respondent gets it — no sign-in, no account — which is the only way to be certain the address you
   are about to circulate works.

The three buttons above the box — **Link**, **QR code** and **Embed** — are three views of the same
link, not three different links. Whichever one you are looking at, it is the same address with the
same settings and the same count. [Share a QR code](qr-code.md) and
[Embed in your own site](embed.md) cover the other two.

## Give it a name, and make more than one

The name is yours. Click it at the top of the panel, type, and press Enter. It is for telling your
own links apart — respondents never see it, and renaming does not change the address.

Press **New**, at the top of the list, for another one. Its tooltip says what that buys you:
**Create another link with its own settings and its own response count**. One for the newsletter and
one for the printed poster, and the count under each name tells you which of them worked. They can
have different closing dates, different limits and different captcha settings — see
[Set limits and dates](limits-and-dates.md).

## If the address gets somewhere it should not have

Under **Settings**, **Access token** deals with a link that has leaked — posted publicly when it was
meant for twenty people, forwarded outside the organisation. Press **Reissue link**, then confirm
with **Replace it**; **Keep it** backs out.

What this does *not* do is change the web address: **The web address stays the same, so printed
links and QR codes keep working.** Only the credential behind it is replaced. Anyone who already had
the form open keeps their session until it expires on its own, and everybody else needs the link
afresh — which, since the address is unchanged, means nothing you have printed is wasted.

## Deleting one

**Delete this share link** is at the foot of the settings, behind a confirmation of **Delete it** and
**Keep it**, and it is exactly as final as it sounds: **The link stops working for anyone who has
it.**

A link that has collected responses cannot be deleted at all. MJ Forms says why: **This link has
responses, so it cannot be deleted — it records where they came from. Turn off "Open to responses"
to retire it.** That switch is the right tool anyway. It stops the link taking answers while keeping
the record of which link each response arrived through, and you can turn it back on later without
reprinting anything.

## What happens next

Answers arrive on the **Responses** tab of the form, and in the count under the link's name on this
tab. The badge beside the link's name is the thing to watch: while it reads **Live**, anyone with
the address can answer.

If it reads anything else, or if people tell you the link does not work,
[Why a link is not working](why-a-link-is-not-working.md) reads every state in turn and names the
button that fixes it.
