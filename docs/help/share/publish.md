# Publish a form

Your questions, exactly as they stand, made answerable — and an honest answer from MJ Forms about
whether anybody can actually reach them.

## Before you start

- The questions written and in the order you want them:
  [Add and arrange questions](../build/add-and-arrange-questions.md).
- One look at the form at phone width, because publishing is the point after which other people see
  it: [Preview on phone and tablet](../design/preview.md).

## What publishing does

Publishing takes a copy of the form as it stands at that moment, and that copy is what respondents
answer.

Everything follows from this. Your edits afterwards go into the form, not into the copy, so a
respondent who opens your link an hour later still gets the copy — the same questions, the same
wording, the same colours — until you publish again. Every copy is kept, and every response
remembers which one it was answering, which is why rewording a question next month never changes
what somebody was actually asked. The word for one of those copies is a
[published version](../reference/glossary.md).

Two things publishing is not. It is not a save — MJ Forms saved each edit as you made it, and there
is no save button anywhere. And it is not a way in: publishing makes the form answerable, and a
[share link](../reference/glossary.md) is what makes it reachable. They are separate acts, which is
the single fact this page exists to get across.

## Publish the form

1. Press **Publish**, at the right-hand end of the bar above the tabs. Its tooltip says what it is
   for: **Publish this form so it can be shared and answered.**
2. Wait while the control reads **Checking…**. MJ Forms is working out where the form now stands.
3. Read the message that appears in the bar, a little to the left of the publish control. After a
   first publish it reads `Published version 1.`, and the number climbs with every publish after
   that.
4. Read the control itself. It has stopped being a button that publishes and has become a report of
   how far the form reaches.

If the form carries broken rules, MJ Forms refuses to publish at all, counts them and reads them
back to you. That refusal and its cure are in [Show, skip and branch](../build/logic.md).

## What the control says afterwards

Four things, and only one of them means people can answer. Hover any of them and the full sentence
appears.

**Published** — **Everything in this form is live on its public link.** Nothing to do. Somebody
could be answering your form right now.

**Published, not shared** — **This form is published, but it has no share link yet, so nobody can
open it. Create one on the Distribute tab to start collecting responses.** Press it: the words are a
button, and pressing them opens the **Distribute** tab.

**Published, not collecting** — **This form is published, but none of its share links are taking
responses. Reopen one on the Distribute tab to start collecting again.** Your links exist and may
well be in circulation; every one of them has stopped. Press it, then find out which state each link
is in: [Why a link is not working](why-a-link-is-not-working.md).

**Published, link unchecked** — **This form is published. Its share links could not be read just
now, so whether anyone can reach it is unknown. Open the Distribute tab to see why.** MJ Forms is
declining to guess rather than showing you a reassuring tick it has not earned. Press it; the
**Distribute** tab carries the real error.

### Published, not shared is where a first form lands

Publish a new form and this is what you get, every time. It reads like a failure and it is not one.

Nothing went wrong, nothing was rejected and the form is fine. Publishing wrote the copy; it did not
hand anybody an address, because MJ Forms has no way of knowing who you meant to send it to. The
whole remedy is [Create a share link](share-link.md), which takes about five seconds on the tab the
button opens for you.

Send the link out before you publish and your respondents get a page reading
**This form hasn't been published yet. If you were sent this link, its author still needs to publish
the form.** — which is the same mistake from the other end, and is why it is worth opening your own
link once before anyone else does.

## What happens next

The moment you edit anything, the control becomes **Publish changes**, with the tooltip
**Your edits are not published yet. Publishing pushes them live.** Everyone holding your link keeps
getting the last published version until you press it. A form can sit like that for as long as you
like; nothing is half-published.

So the rhythm of working on a live form is: edit freely, preview, then publish when the change is
one you are happy for people to be answering.
